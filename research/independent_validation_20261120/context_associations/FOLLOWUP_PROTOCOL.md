# Animal prediction and physical seal matching: execution protocol

Frozen on 2026-11-23 before inspecting additional candidate animal outcomes or acquiring candidate photographs in this follow-up. Existing observations remain discovery data, including Cúper's photo checks.

1. **Animal prediction.** On a securely excavated copper tablet, sign 923 predicts elephant-related iconography on the other face. This tests a semantic category, not the sound or the exact proposed word “ivory/tusk.” Absence of 923 does not predict absence of an elephant. Ordinary seals are outside this primary test: Cúper already tested that transfer. Other tablet materials will be reported separately.
2. Exclude the known `706-033-923-740` family, including terminal `001`, `705/706` variants, one replacement/insertion/deletion and connected near-variants. Exclude incomplete texts whose family cannot be distinguished. More copies or catalogue duplicates of this family count as zero new trials. Previously inspected animal outcomes are discovery observations, never a blind holdout.
3. Search existing second-catalogue/new-event transcriptions, then published primary copper-tablet inventories and excavated finds. Record the candidate denominator, source identities, known-family exclusions and missing joins. A different catalogue is not by itself a new archaeological sample. No eligible newly identified family means no prediction test; do not calculate a success rate or p-value.
4. **Physical matches.** Start with the 15 exact-text pairs already identified by Cúper. Separate his prior L-161/M-895 image rejection and related Lothal copies from newly examined pairs. Recover photographs of the particular objects. Check iconography and relative glyph spacing, placement and carving details, allowing mirroring and photographic distortion. Identical transcription, animal species or overall visual resemblance cannot establish one physical die. Record a diagnostic mismatch, a physically supported match, or unresolved evidence, with the actual observations.
5. **Issuer versus contents.** Run this comparison only after a physical match and independently identified package contents/use contexts are available. Cane, cloth or wood backing identifies a support/package feature, not its contents. Neither proposed translations nor generic site craft activity supplies an independent contents label. If this prerequisite fails, report that the issuer-versus-contents experiment remains unperformed.
