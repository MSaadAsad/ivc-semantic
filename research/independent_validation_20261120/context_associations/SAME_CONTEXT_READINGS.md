# Other inscriptions on the same object types at the same locations

**The proposed vocabulary is mixed even within the same recorded area and object type.** The useful comparison includes the other inscriptions, rather than only selecting marks whose proposed meanings resemble their carrier.

## Correcting the rice comparison

There are three different things that must not be merged:

1. **Older English rice wording:** H-1740/H-1742 contain `540-540`, the measure mark twice. They do not contain proposed rice signs 215/216. The earlier “Two rice measures” English wording is therefore not evidence of a separate rice sign or of actual rice contents.
2. **A proposed rice sign:** M-1599 has `480-175-215`; the current key assigns rice to 215, while the earlier complete sentence still reads “Seed-weight, tax, [215].” It is recorded at Mohenjo-daro, SD, excavation **SD 2976**. It is the only pot with the exact `SD-` area field in this catalogue, so no same-area pot control is available. Other Mohenjo-daro pots are broader city comparisons.
3. **Measured rice contents:** none of these objects has a verified residue/starch analysis linked in the preceding feasibility investigation.

The older English rice wording also occurs on **a bangle**, H-2574: `[000]-540`, “[lost sign], rice measure,” at Harappa E Trench 54. This is a cross-object comparison from a different trench, not an MN or F Trench 43 local neighbour. It shows that the proposal itself uses this measure word on an object that is not a rice container.

## What this means

The mixed vocabulary is compatible with words occurring in names, affiliations, measures or administrative formulas. It does not establish those functions or identify the language. It does show why **a matching word on a matching object is insufficient by itself**: the same proposed lexicon also produces measure, cloth, metal, tax and sale words on bangles, and house/star/numerical marks on pots. A specific reading needs a prediction that distinguishes those possible uses.

## Scope and reproduction

All **685 Lipi bangle/pot face records** were combined by catalogue object ID into **49 bangles and 629 pots**. No Mahadevan duplicate corpus is added. Twelve selected comparison groups cover **126 distinct objects**; their **148 memberships overlap**, because the 22 stamped F Trench 43 pots are also in the 38-pot all-subtype group. All remaining bangle/pot objects remain in the full 678-object CSV, including unknown readings and missing findspots.

Existing sentences are retained verbatim, with current literal key values separate. Broad MN/HR/DK areas are not described as exact rooms. No new meaning assignments, translations or significance tests were made. This is a complete descriptive contrast inventory, not a claim that earlier researchers had never noticed these readings.
