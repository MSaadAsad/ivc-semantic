Patterns in the actual records

  • Goblet/corner 440: all 12 Harappa pottery examples have the one-sign text 440. Seven are recorded at F Trench 43 and one at AB Trench 39N, all eight with phase C / period 3 fields; four have no detailed area. Among stamped pots specifically, 12/50 at Harappa carry 440 versus 0/22 at Mohenjo-daro. The thirteenth pottery example is a longer Mohenjo-daro graffito with an older “Received...” reading.
  • Capacity measure 540: H-1740 and H-1742 both repeat 540-540 at F Trench 43, phase C / period 3. Four other pots have only 540; M-1379 has a longer “Month-Drum house” reading. This distribution crosses three cities.
  • Rod sign 838: all four rods are in the DK/DKG areas of Mohenjo-daro. They repeat two formulas, each on two rods. Mohenjo-daro already supplies 32/42 rod faces in the catalogue; the four examples cannot alone establish a special city-level association.
  • Bangle and pair/pact: certain examples occur at both Harappa and Mohenjo-daro. The local object frequencies are 2/7 versus 4/40 for 790, and 2/7 versus 7/40 for 840. More finds at Mohenjo-daro do not mean a higher local rate: it supplies 40 of the 49 bangle objects (41/50 faces).
  • Gold weight 645: only M-2129 contributes; it is recorded at DK-E, Trench E1. There is no second positive gold specimen or multi-city comparison.

Repeated formulas and common findspots may reflect production or administrative practices. They do not by themselves establish particular Dravidian words, a workshop's function, simultaneous deposition or stamping from one physical die. The local patterns above are descriptive; no new significance test was run.

Prior work is credited: jobs/out/goods.md already records six/nine bangles, four rod examples and twelve single-sign Harappa pot marks; metrology_ledger.md studies vessel marks; the continuation's earlier word/context screen covers many site and layer comparisons. This release supplies full translations/findspots and reconciles the screenshot, rather than claiming those associations were newly discovered.
