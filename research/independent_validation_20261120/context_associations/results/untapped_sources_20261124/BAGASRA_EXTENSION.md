# Bagasra sherds and the Eo2 clay sealing

24 November 2026. The excavation register adds context for 73 sherds beyond the 16 previously inspected in the figures. A separately published clay impression gives one additional inscription for the corpus backlog.

## A six-sign sealing with a thread impression

The Archaeological Survey of India describes a Phase II clay sealing from **trench Eo2** with a unicorn, **six characters on two lines**, and a thread impression near the break. The national museum register lists **UM-MSU-GJ-343702**, an inscribed terracotta sealing from the 2004 Bagasra season, Eo2, layer 3, depth 120 cm. The year, trench, material and object description make this a likely join. The ASI description lacks a museum number and the museum entry lacks a sign count. The join remains provisional. [ASI 2003–04 report, printed pp. 37–38, Plate 21](https://asi.nic.in/admin/publications/ePublicationReportsDetails/download/115) · [National Mission on Monuments and Antiquities catalogue](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/exploreObject.do?object=78).

The excavators' group photograph in *Antiquity* shows the clay sealing at the center. The animal body and raised strokes above it are visible. The available 325 × 444-pixel image leaves the upper signs and row boundaries too ambiguous to encode. The reference Lipi table has six Bagasra faces, all on seals or seal fragments. The photographed clay object is an additional source candidate outside that set. [Excavators' Fig. 8 and photograph](https://www.antiquity.ac.uk/projgall/bhan304) · [Full-size figure](https://antiquity.ac.uk/sites/default/files/projgall/bhan304/images/figure8big.jpg).

I matched all four surrounding objects by their designs and breaks to Jamison et al.'s accessioned photographs. Positions below refer to Fig. 8 rotated clockwise so the four seal animals stand upright. The three complete faces have one line each. Given the excavators' two-line description for the sealing, none of those three fits as the stamp. The fourth seal preserves too little of its text to settle the comparison. The photograph gives no positive die match or new translation. The thread impression independently supports use as a fastening and supplies no package contents. [Comparative seal photographs, Figs. 7–8 and 10–11](https://heritageuniversityofkerala.com/JournalPDF/Volume5/1.pdf#page=10).

The 1996–97 ASI report separately describes three damaged sealings with rod-and-cord impressions on their backs. One has five impressions attributed to the same 1.4 cm square unicorn seal, with faint signs. A second carries a grid and a third spiral designs. The description supplies a promising repeat-image source for the first piece. Its accession and relationship to the previously catalogued Bagasra fastening examples remain open. The older finds are outside the 2004 Eo2 candidate join. [ASI 1996–97 report, Plate IX](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/NAS1/nmma_doc/IAR/Indian%20Archaeology%201996_97%20A%20Review.pdf).

It includes the study ID, museum number where given, phase, trench, layer, depth, mark position, author-assessed sign count and the figure for each previously viewed sherd. The strongest remaining pottery targets are **MSU 7400** (study 41, Phase III, Eo2 layer 3 at 90 cm) and **MSU 7078** (study 9, Eb4 layer 4 at 140 cm, phase unknown). Both are red-slipped jar-pot fragments explicitly labelled as Indus script and recorded with two signs. The notes add a black band for 7078. Two further Indus-script rows allow one or two signs, MSU **5420** (study 65) and **7415** (study 14). The accessible thesis and museum searches did not yield a photograph or drawing linked to any of these four study IDs. ## What the excavation context adds to the two proposed meanings

- **“Two quarter-shares” on BSJ 101.** Lindstrom's earlier sign chart independently gives a wheel beside two strokes among the Phase II finds outside the wall. The excavation report places trench Eo10 about 45 m south of the fortification and records stone weights in its Phase II habitation deposits. This gives a plausible setting for a quantity label on a long-distance storage jar. No weight or measured quantity has been joined to the jar itself. [Lindstrom, Fig. 6.2](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=370) · [ASI 2002–03, printed pp. 64–66](https://asi.nic.in/admin/publications/ePublicationReportsDetails/download/112).
- **“Four woven-cloths” or “four new-cloths” on the 6606 bowl.** The same chart depicts a branched form with four strokes for Phase III outside the wall. The vessel table gives a **15–17 cm external rim diameter**. Museum entries place a hammerstone (330587), copper object (327512) and copper rod (327523) at the bowl's recorded **Eo11, layer 4, 105 cm**, and a bangle (366626) at 90–105 cm in the same layer. The spindle whorl found in Eo11 (325563) is from **layer 2 at 25 cm**. This comparison gives a mixed activity context and no independent textile association for the mark. A trench, layer and depth match alone gives proximity, with no documented shared use or feature. [Bowl vessel table, printed p. 472](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=491) · [Museum household goods](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/exploreMusObject.do?musname=UM-MSU&object=55) · [Copper and spindle whorl entries](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/exploreObject.do?object=78) · [Bangle entry](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/exploreObject.do?object=42&pager.offset=231500).

The chart corroborates the graphic identifications for the two proposed readings. Its sketches are comparisons drawn from Parpola and omit the study IDs, so they add no individually imaged specimens. The [existing sequence comparisons](READING_TESTS.md) supply the linguistic proposals. The independent finds give the Eo2 sealing a physical use clue and narrow the surviving pottery targets. The encoded corpus and proposed translations stay as recorded.
