## Rakhigarhi lead ingot

Nath, Law and Garge publish a photograph of the complete plano-convex lead ingot and separate detail photographs of the marks on both faces. They assayed the same object. Table 3 gives the ratios ²⁰⁸Pb/²⁰⁷Pb **2.4677**, ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁶Pb **0.84747**, and ²⁰⁷Pb/²⁰⁴Pb **15.682**. The authors place it within the isotope field for southern Balochistan lead ores. The specimen is from RGR-2. RGR 424 is a small prism-shaped lead object from RGR-4 shown separately in Fig. 13. The excavation draft's Appendix 3, Fig. 14, reproduces the same ingot images and assay. A 1998–99 preliminary excavation article describes a plano-convex inscribed lead ingot among that season's finds. [Materials study, Fig. 12 and Table 3](https://heritageuniversityofkerala.com/JournalPDF/Volume2/74-100.pdf#page=14) · [1998–99 excavation account, p. 49](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/85200.pdf) · [Excavation draft](https://rarebooksocietyofindia.org/book_archive/196174216674_10153813006761675.pdf).

- **Top, Fig. 12C.** The sharpest form is an elongated, closed body with a tail-like cluster at one end. There are also short strokes and angular incisions. The form is worth checking against the plain fish family, including 220. The photograph and published red tracing leave boundaries and cutting direction uncertain.
- **Bottom, Fig. 12D.** The tracing shows a nested angular figure with branching strokes inside, alongside larger branching and angular forms. The photograph is worn. Neither side has been assigned a complete sequence.
- **Findspot and existing coverage.** The nearest description in ch. 8 is a Mature Harappan semicircular, two-sided lead piece from RGR-2 trench Q-19/4. The draft gives stroke counts on each face that are difficult to reconcile with the published tracing, and neither description supplies a common accession. RGR-2 is established for the assayed ingot. The Q-19/4 join remains a candidate. The working Lipi catalogue contains no face with lead as its recorded material. Rakhigarhi face 5559.1, `220-706` (“Star, kalam”), has unknown material, two recorded signs and no accession. No image or metadata supports a match to the ingot. The earlier context study used Nath et al. for a steatite analysis and did not register this ingot.

If the clearly visible fish-like mark proves to be 220, Cúper's proposed reading is *mīn*, “star.” Sign 220 appears on 467 of 5,679 Lipi faces, including 32 pottery faces and 15 faces with recorded copper. A fish-shaped sign on this object is compatible with use in a name, designation or other text on metal. It tells us little about whether the value was “star,” and the provenance assay does not identify a trade destination. The ingot is a new target for assessing a metal or quantity reading against an independently characterised object.

## Bagasra J1 and Mohenjo-daro

Used only the excavator's description of J1 to define the screen. The main case is an inverted V, two upright lines and a fish-like sign with side strokes. The scan checks both orientations. The two lines can appear as one two-stroke code or as two single-stroke codes. In the broader pass, several caret and fish variants are allowed. The author reports two or three characters, so a ligature or mixed mark remains possible. The full-size Bagasra Figs. 1, 13 and 14 were not retrievable in this pass. [Bagasra paper, description on printed p. 187 and Figs. 13–14](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333220424_The_Cultural_and_Stratigraphic_Context_of_the_Harappan_Black_Slipped_Jar_at_Bagasra_Gujarat).

| Search across 5,679 Lipi faces | Candidate strings across both orientations | Hits |
|---|---:|---:|
| Plain caret, two strokes, fish with side strokes | 72 | 0 |
| Expanded caret and fish variants, two strokes | 640 | 0 |
| Expanded variants, one stroke | 320 | 1 |

The one-stroke comparison is **M-2061**, Mackay LXIII:6, accession DK7286, face 2161.1. It begins `480-001-235` (“Seed-weight; One Bamboo-star”) and continues `482-ALT-742` (“Hill-share, [unclear sign], second-ward house”) in the existing key. The fish variant here has a caret-like addition in the catalogue. The comparison finds an analogous arrangement, while the published descriptions differ over both the count and the fish details. The two-stroke screens supply no evidence for a repeated complete formula. [Mackay's original chapter, Pot-marks and Inscriptions](https://indike.org/pramana/inp.archaeology.monograph.mackay-1938.v1.c10#v1.c10.s11.1).

One find-number cross-reference also changes how to read the older comparisons. Mackay explicitly identifies **plate LVII:36** as a facsimile of the rim on **LVII:42**, whose catalogue accession is DK12585 / M-2060. Face 2166.1 currently gives the former plate without an object number and proposes “Month, of, [703]; Four [067]; [lost sign].” Face 2165.2 proposes “2 kāṇam-shares — Of the Tax/Tribute House” for DK12585. The printed cross-reference supports attaching 2166.1 to the same **jar**. Whether its signs overlap any other recorded face still needs direct plate comparison. Mackay says **LVII:35** is the separate rim sign on **LVII:41** (DK12403 / M-2064), even though the working accession field for DK12585 contains `42+35`. Keep that disagreement when comparing the readings. The old inscriptions on LXIII:3 and LXIII:6 were already linked in our capacity study.

## Rakhigarhi pottery

The full excavation draft confirms that the table's dedicated drawing column is blank. Digitized [all 163 numbered rows](rakhigarhi_rgr1_graffiti.csv) from printed pp. 208–217. This preserves one row for each numbered fragment, including entries with the same locus, layer and depth. Vessel forms are supplied for 25. The table records **152 after firing and 11 before firing**. These are marked sherds selected into a table, with no denominator for all pottery and no per-row mark placement. Eight named loci contain examples of both techniques. At L-5/1+2, layer 10, 220 cm, the table records a basin marked after firing and a separate fragment of unspecified form marked before. The recovered PDF also supplies the report's images elsewhere, including the ingot in its appendix. No drawing or accession was recovered for the seven-shape bone from Y 24/4. [Nath's excavation draft, pp. 205–220 and 380–381](https://rarebooksocietyofindia.org/book_archive/196174216674_10153813006761675.pdf#page=205).
