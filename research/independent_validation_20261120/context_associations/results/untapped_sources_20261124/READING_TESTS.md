# Reading tests on the new marked objects

24 November 2026. The catalogue was held fixed. Sign candidates come from the published photographs and drawings, with comparisons against the repo's sign list and Key 6A. Counts use contiguous coded signs and the catalogue's reading order. Counts of repeated faces and whole matches are given separately. The objects under review were never inserted into the search corpus.

## BSR J101 black-slipped jar

The drawing shows **two separate upright strokes to the left of a pointed oval with six spokes**. The latter is a good match to Mahadevan 391 and Lipi 820. The jar stands out among the recovered Bagasra material because the count and the wheel are separately visible. From the right, the visible pair is `820-032` (or `820-002` with the short-stroke code). Both stroke codes carry “two” in the key. Keeping the current value of the wheel gives **“two quarter-shares”** (*kāl iru*). Nothing in the marks specifies what the shares were of or how many litres the jar held. The author allows a third sign, and the drawing ends at a break. [Lindstrom 2013, Fig. 6.9, panel 9](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=384).

The underlying accession has support from both reports. Lindstrom's caption duplicates panel label 9 for BSJ 129. Her separate Fig. 6.15 identifies BSJ 129 as a rim with a short run of strokes. The Fig. 6.9 panel shows a body sherd and matches the Appendix C record for BSJ 101, trench Eo10, layer 7, 235 cm, Phase II, outside the wall. Ajithprasad's Table 4 independently lists these same excavation fields for BSR J101 (panel 9). This identifies the recovered drawing with J101 while retaining the caption error. [Appendix C and Fig. 6.15](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=485) · [Ajithprasad, Table 4](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333220424_The_Cultural_and_Stratigraphic_Context_of_the_Harappan_Black_Slipped_Jar_at_Bagasra_Gujarat).

| Reference | What the comparison supplies | Proposed wording in the repo |
|---|---|---|
| H-1882, Harappa, faience tablet | Whole text `820-002`, using the short pair of strokes | Two quarter-shares. The other face is read “Three kāṇam-shares.” |
| H-799, Harappa, faience tablet | Closes with the same tall-stroke form `820-032` | Branch-Chief, two quarter-shares. |
| K-431, Kalibangan, pottery | Starts with `820-002`, followed by a damaged group | Two quarters, kindred, two missing signs. |
| BSR 6719, Bagasra, seal, face 5216.1 | Same wheel with **three** strokes, in the opposite order `033-820` | Manager of the Three-Quarter Rayed-Star Branch-Chief House. |

The Lipi reference has **10 faces on nine objects** with `820-032` and **101 faces on 100 objects** with `820-002`. Their respective reverse orders occur on zero and one face. This supports wheel-before-number for the recovered pair under catalogue conventions. The whole H-1882 text gives a close known counterpart. The recurrence makes “two quarter-shares” a usable comparison on a newly examined jar. It does not independently determine whether the unit is a quarter-share, an alternative counted unit, or part of a designation. There is no independent quantity or contents measurement for J101.

## BSR 6606 Sorath bowl

Lindstrom's **Graffiti 2** shows four adjacent upright marks and a separate branched form on their left. Her table counts two signs, and links this figure by study number to Eo11, layer 4, depth 105 cm, Phase III, outside the wall. The appendix also uses 6606 for a body sherd under study number 30, so the figure match uses **study number 2 and the rim description**. The branch resembles the group mapped to Mahadevan 169. Lipi 405 and 407 both map to M169 in the repo, so the drawing leaves the readings **“four woven-cloths”** and **“four new-cloths”** open. The common four-stroke forms `004` and `034` are both allowed. [Fig. 6.10 and Appendix C](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=385).

Four followed by 405 or 407 gives **18 Lipi faces**. The reverse gives zero. Two are whole inscriptions. **Ra’s al-Junayz face 3865.1** is a jar graffito with `004-405` (“Four woven-cloths”), and **H-518** is a Harappa tablet with `004-407` (“Four new-cloths”). Those comparisons support the order if the branched sign is in this family. The analogous mark on a domestic bowl, an export jar and a tablet makes the proposed phrase usable across different supports. No independent fibre, cloth or four-item count is recorded on this bowl. The mark could also be a tally and a different kind of label. The full clause and the choice between the two sign variants remain unresolved.

## Bagasra J1 and BSR J129

The recovered J1 inset has several converging or crossing grooves near a break. It does not make the boundaries of a caret, pair of strokes and decorated fish clear enough for an exact coding. Under that segmentation, the current key would yield a **measure sign, “two,” and a fish-family reading**, which could be “rayed star” if the fish is 240. The earlier one-stroke comparison, M-2061, begins `480-001-235`. The **current** key assigns 480 *tūtai* (vessel or metal measure) and 235 *oḷi-mīṉ* (bright star). The archived English catalogue says “Seed-weight; One Bamboo-star,” reflecting a different version of the key. There is no full two-stroke and fish match in the earlier screen. When the fish is omitted, `480-032` occurs on two pottery faces, **L-222** and **Ns-79**. The CISI photograph of L-222 confirms the visual sequence of caret, two upright strokes and a divided diamond in right-to-left order. Its existing line is read “Two seed-weights; Four shares,” with the older 480 gloss. These support a quantity context for one component of J1 without supplying a complete reading. [BSJ J1 drawing](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=384) · [Lothal photograph, printed p. 291](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/76884.pdf#page=327) · [Prior exact search](FOLLOWUP.md#bagasra-j1-and-mohenjo-daro).

BSR J129's rim is a useful local comparison with Kalba's pair of slanted incisions. Its drawing gives three longer strokes flanked by two short strokes. Lindstrom documents **54 of 57 simple incised marks on rims**, with **33 of 57** consisting of one or two lines, and treats the group as potentially different from writing. BSR J101 and 6606 have a separate structured sign alongside their strokes on the vessel exterior. Their numerals have more support than attempts to assign units from the rim strokes alone. [Lindstrom 2013, Fig. 6.15 and pp. 372–373](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=391) · [Kalba source and sample comparison](FOLLOWUP.md#kalba-and-the-residue-samples).

The remaining [plate inventory](candidate_readings.csv) captures shapes, scope and unresolved sign boundaries. The two painted Bagasra sherds are distinct from the similarly numbered black-slipped jars. Their motifs have multiple parallels in Lindstrom's Fig. 6.7. The photographed surface-find dish from Shikarpur preserves two strokes plus two worn angular forms. The working catalogue already has several painted Shikarpur entries without object IDs, so this photograph is retained without claiming a ninth object or interpreting its angular forms from the two strokes alone. [Painted finds, Figs. 6.6–6.8](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/TPUFRSAYMWP3T8Y/R/file-e6a9e.pdf#page=379).

## Rakhigarhi lead and marked bone

The top face of the lead ingot has a closed fish-shaped outline with crossed tail strokes and no clearly distinct head ornament. Of the examined fish variants, **220 (Mahadevan 59)** gives the closest plain form. The tentative value under Key 6A is **“star”** (*mīṉ*). Other strokes lack secure grouping and the direction of the composition remains open. “One star” is only a sensitivity case if the nearby groove is a single numeral in the same line. The pairing occurs on **15 existing faces** in either order, 14 seals and one clay pot (Kd-7). No complete text or verified lead-material reading follows from the candidate. The isotope-characterised lead and a fish-shaped mark are independently observable. The specimen provides no support for a rule that the plain fish always pictures the material of its carrier, conditional on the sign match. It leaves cargo, issuer and name readings open. [Nath et al., Fig. 12](https://heritageuniversityofkerala.com/JournalPDF/Volume2/74-100.pdf#page=14).

For the polished bone from Y 24/4, tested the excavator's seven reported shapes **I N I X I X X** by equality alone, in both directions. This requires the same three signs to repeat three, three and one times and in the indicated positions. Among 728 eligible Lipi windows and 651 eligible Mahadevan windows, both the exact repetition pattern and the looser three/three/one count had zero hits. These catalogues overlap, so the counts are reported separately. The source records letter-like shapes rather than verified sign boundaries. The comparison gives no existing text to read from without a photograph. [Excavation draft, printed p. 219](https://rarebooksocietyofindia.org/book_archive/196174216674_10153813006761675.pdf#page=219).
