## Bagasra black-slipped jars

Imported [all ten mark-bearing fragments from Table 4](bagasra_marked_jars.csv), each with an accession, trench, layer, depth and phase. The working catalogue has six Bagasra faces, all seals (BSR 7368, 2037, 6719, 6952, 7197 and 8288). Prior work covers Bagasra seals and a fastening impression. Searches of the local reports and scripts for the jar paper, author and distinctive accessions did not find an import of this marked series. These are ten added **object records with published markings**. The figure is linked. Its image file could not be retrieved for independent sign coding in this pass. [Ajithprasad 2013, pp. 187–191, Tables 3–4 and Figures 13–14](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333220424_The_Cultural_and_Stratigraphic_Context_of_the_Harappan_Black_Slipped_Jar_at_Bagasra_Gujarat), [Figure 13](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Graffiti-on-the-black-slipped-jars-from-Bagasra_fig7_333220424).

- **BSR J126**, Phase I, trench Eo3, layer 8, 210 cm depth, southern edge outside the fortification. The author reports three signs on a nearly complete rim.
- **BSR J1**, Phase II, trench Do7, layer 5, shell-working debris inside the fortification. The report describes an inverted V, two upright strokes and an embellished fish-like form on the shoulder. The prose and Table 4 disagree on the bastion's corner and the table reports depth as `50+65` cm. Neither a sign code nor a reading direction is assigned here.
- **Placement over time**. Three Phase I fragments carry rim marks (one also on its shoulder). The six Phase II fragments have the marks described on shoulders or bodies. The single Phase III sherd has rim marks with uncertain script status. Ajithprasad already described this pattern and proposed that an accessible shoulder could be marked after the rim was covered. The phase and fragment-type differences limit that inference.

Figure 14 offers exact publication and plate pointers to proposed visual parallels at Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Nausharo and Ra’s al-Junayz. The author already compares sign shapes. An ordered, independently coded match on the same vessel form across locations could test whether a formula travels with that form. Kalba provides a candidate rim-mark comparison. Table 3 gives site-level rim and throat ranges. The paper reports that height varied independently of those measures. There is no specimen-level volume measurement for these ten pieces, so they supply zero new trials of a proposed capacity unit. [Ajithprasad 2013, pp. 184–191](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333220424_The_Cultural_and_Stratigraphic_Context_of_the_Harappan_Black_Slipped_Jar_at_Bagasra_Gujarat).

## Arabian residue samples and the object join

The 2025 residue study releases 179 rows, including **32 sherds identified as Indus black-slipped jars**. Imported those 32 rows with identifiers, contexts, jar grouping, fragment portions and selected measurements. They come from Salūt-ST1 (18), Kalba 4 (7), Hili 8 (4), Bat (2) and Dahwa 7 (1). They include multiple fragments attributed to the same vessel and previously published Hili and Salūt samples. Each source record is a sherd. [Study and S4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0324661), [Original S4 CSV](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0324661.s004), [32-row extract](gulf_indus_jar_samples.csv).

| Tested piece | Recorded context | Published residue interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| HIA802AE / A0802 | Hili 8, UAE, 750 UF59, Wadi Suq phase III | Plant oil |
| HIA1064AE / A1064 | Hili 8, UAE, context or ID 119, Umm an-Nar IIf | Plant oil |
| SLT63, vessel BSJ 3 | Salūt-ST1, Oman, context 330, Umm an-Nar | Dairy fats |
| SLT40 | Salūt-ST1, Oman, context 330, local beige sandy ware rim with graffiti | Degraded animal fats |

The two plant-oil sherds are the same samples reported in the 2022 Hili study. The plant species and original cargo remain open. The 2024 Salūt study documents SLT40 as a marked rim, which the 2025 dataset identifies as local ware. Its marks lack an assigned Indus sign value. The 32 Indus rows supply **zero verified inscription–residue joins**. No marking is documented in those spreadsheet fields, and the working catalogue contains no pottery face at those five sites. The 2025 chemically diverse assemblage supports using specimen-level contents rather than assigning one commodity to the jar type. [Hili 8 sample register and analysis](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A5ac39a06-2f55-4669-b904-feaf557a2e70/files/ssf2687245), [Salūt-ST1 paper, §§2.2 and 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/aae.12247), [Regional analysis, §5.4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0324661).

**Kalba 4 has the closest further lead.** The excavators illustrate a marked Indus storage-jar rim in Fig. 12/8 from secondary ditch fill `53.161`. The residue table has two black-slipped jar bodies, K4.828 and K4.830, from `53/159` and `53/160`. They are distinct recorded contexts and no join, refit or shared vessel ID is published in the sources inspected. The report discusses an ownership-mark interpretation for the rim incisions. The one Kalba object in the working corpus is a cylinder seal. [Eddisford and Phillips 2009, pp. 106–108](https://www.academia.edu/1469079/Eddisford_and_Phillips_2009_-_Kalba_in_the_third_millennium), [Kalba imported ceramics study](https://doi.org/10.1111/aae.12208), [Residue S4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0324661.s004).
