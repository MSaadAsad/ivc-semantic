Dholavira: two context links supported by source images and findspots

23 November 2026 · completed documentary review · saved locally

Two existing objects now have usable documentary context links: a box attachment and textile contact. We reviewed 21 source/object possibilities. Nineteen remain unresolved. No new inscription or translation was created.

This links a published accession-labelled image or sign drawing to an existing coded inscription using visible signs and recorded excavation context. It is not a comparison of two independently authenticated photographs of the physical specimen. The two links remain excluded from a claim of fresh predictive semantic validation.

4608 ↔ object 4156: box attachment

Existing proposal: “two quarters: [lost sign], chief.”

The 2023 paper explicitly names ASI 4608 beside this drawing. Its display order matches 520–000–002–820; Cúper's analytical order for 4156.1 is 820–002–000–520. Bailey, XH 34, 45 cm, red complete sealing, unicorn and two impressions also agree.

The excavation report infers a square/rectangular box from the reverse. This attaches a documented box-use context to the existing proposed reading. A box can carry an authority's mark, a quantity or other information: it does not establish “chief” or “quarter-share.” The second encoded impression, 4156.2, inherits object context only.

Bisht 2015, printed p319; Mukhopadhyay 2023, discussion of ASI 4608.

20502 ↔ object 4292: textile contact

Existing reading: [604] — unresolved.

The source photo beside accession 20502 preserves a ladder-shaped sign compatible with 604. South Reservoir, 48×45×4, stratum 7, 312 cm, a grey fragment and one surviving sign agree with 4292.1.

The report identifies textile impressions on the back. We attach textile contact, not “cloth commodity” or a translation “cloth.” The sign could label an issuer, authorization, amount, contents or something else; this object alone does not distinguish them.

Bisht 2015, printed p323. The source page placement and front/back photographs were inspected; no separate accession-labelled corpus photograph was recovered.

What the other comparisons changed

The proposed vessel-linked object 4289 has a source/catalogue period disagreement and different sign counts. Object 4300, currently coded 923 and proposed “Ivory,” is described by the excavator as a half fragment whose mark might be a symbol rather than an Indus sign. Neither supplies a new confirmed commodity reading. Face 3951.1 still has two competing source candidates at 320 cm.
