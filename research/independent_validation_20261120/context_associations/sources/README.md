# Sources recovered for the animal/die follow-up

`die_images/` contains 20 unchanged catalogue-image reproductions already tracked in the upstream repository. Original paths and the fixed upstream commit are recorded in `results/physical_photo_sources.csv`. A seal's catalogue image may show its modern impression; it is not an ancient package impression. Orientation and image scale must be checked individually before geometric die registration. No such registration is claimed in this work.

Primary publications inspected:

- [Frenez 2020, *Mirrored signs*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5513821): printed pp22–23 identify L-6/L-201, L-6/L-208 and L-37/L-210; figure1 illustrates two links. Table2 part2, p32, supplies the object-specific support/context labels transcribed here. PDF pages2,3,9,11,12 were rendered and visually checked. The downloaded paper is held in the adjacent source cache rather than redistributed in this repository.
- [Frenez 2024, *Were the Cretulae … Part of an Administrative Archive?*](https://doi.org/10.1080/02666030.2024.2401206): the discussion of figure11 describes L-201/L-208 through an undiscovered intervening seal, while figure11 still includes L-6/L-208. This differs from the direct link explicitly stated in 2020. The discrepancy is retained; L-6/L-208 is not silently promoted to independent confirmation.
- [Parpola 2008, *Copper Tablets from Mohenjo-daro and the Study of the Indus Script*](https://tuhat.helsinki.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/127257447/Parpola_A_2008._Copper_tablets_from_Mohenjo_daro._ECL_During_Caspers_Vol.pdf): pp133–135 describe the typology and the B6/B12 shared-inscription connection. These existing families are not a fresh corpus. Full primary text was available through the web tool; direct local download returned403. No new individual specimen accession was recovered from this paper.

The external search was bounded, not an exhaustive survey of every later excavation. The second-catalogue/new-event and raw copper tables are already in the repository; different catalogue records are never treated as new archaeological objects merely because they occur in another file.

What is new here is the explicit source-to-object join and candidate eligibility/image review. The physical identifications and the general observation of cross-container seal use were already published by Parpola/Frenez. Earlier repository text in `jobs/out/beads_cotton.md` said object-specific backing information could not be recovered freely; the open2020 table supplies some of that information, but no commodity contents.
