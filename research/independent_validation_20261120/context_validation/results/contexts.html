What do the inscriptions say where we can recover the archaeological context?

22 November 2026 · Primary-source context recovery. Every translation below is an existing proposal; no words have been changed to fit a workshop.

Thirty existing catalogue objects now have accession-linked source annotations. The useful distinction is between a reported workshop, a find near one, a house, and a displaced surface find.

What this establishes: better documented locations and comparison cases. Collection was retrospective; the curator knew the readings and there are no independent ratings. Near a workshop does not mean that a seal names its product. The data do not yet provide secure craft-specific inscription groups for a comparative semantic test.

Three concrete corrections

Bagasra: BSR2037 and BSR8288 are near the faience/lithic area at similar reported depths. Keep them together as one vicinity cluster. Shell-associated BSR5555/6952 have lost the readable inscription; they cannot validate a bangle word. BSR7197 is probably redeposited near the gateway.

Harappa: eleven objects belong to three rooms in one house. A small oven or kiln and domestic debris do not identify a textile or pottery workshop. Surface bangle H-2576 must not be assigned to an in-situ household activity.

Rakhigarhi: RGR6304 is from mound RGR-2, while the bead factory with two reported elephant sealings is on RGR-1. The elephant-tag candidate is not joined without an accession or photographic identity check.
