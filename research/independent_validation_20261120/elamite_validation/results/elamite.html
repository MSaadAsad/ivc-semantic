Could Elamite explain the Indus inscriptions?

22 November 2026 · Dataset coverage and a restricted rebus check. Scope corrected after the user's clarification.

New direct sequence experiment: Elamite-style written-agreement prediction uses raw Indus sequences without proposed words or meanings. This page preserves the earlier dictionary work and its scope correction.

This extension does not supply a full Elamite–Indus test. An Elamite reading must be allowed to use Elamite words and sounds, without resembling Cúper’s proposed Dravidian pronunciations. His individual meanings are also hypotheses, not independently established translations.

The relevant completed component: test whether Elamite can reproduce the particular picture/meaning wordplay proposed in the existing work. Only12/48 principal pairs have both meanings covered; none supplies the specified equal-form bridge. This limited result does not exclude an Elamite reading of Indus.

Elamite vocabulary is allowed to differ

The release lists a-a-in “house” (N:4, nE), da-la “tax” (N:1093, nE), and su-un-ki-ir “king-SG” (V:1505, mE). If an Indus sign had one of these meanings in Elamite, the Elamite form could be entirely different from a proposed Dravidian reading. These dictionary entries do not themselves assign that meaning to an Indus sign.

Period key: oE = Old Elamite, mE = Middle Elamite, nE = Neo-Elamite, achE = Achaemenid Elamite. Other source codes remain as recorded. SG means singular.

What this adds

Earlier repository tests already used Elamite dictionaries and tested proposed Linear-Elamite sound transfer. This extension adds the structured DigitalPasts input, period/borrowing-flag splits and fixed-form/same-meaning affinity controls. The source derives from Hinz–Koch; it is not an independent ancient corpus. No source-verified historical Elamo-Dravidian sound-correspondence test or independent Indus semantic assignment is claimed. All 163 signs are retained in the scope inventory, with untested compounds/allographs distinguished.
