# Executive verdict and final assessment

The project has produced a substantial, testable collection of sign-pattern and archaeological hypotheses. **The available evidence does not yet establish its precise meanings, phonetic values, complete translations, or a uniquely identified Proto-Dravidian language.** Its most defensible advance is a linked exploratory research system; the gap is independent semantic identification and prediction.

The continuation tests the latest **163-sign** inventory, beyond the earlier 114-sign state in the supplied brief and beyond the November 19 audit of Key #4. It preserves positive and negative results:

- **Confirmed in these computations:** real sign–artifact patterns; 22/540 contrasts survive the stated site-stratified familywise permutation control. The literal inventory applications and coverage counts are reproducible.
- **Promising, conditional evidence:** the expanded 163-sign semantic grouping beats 199 constrained rival groupings at the Monte Carlo floor, p=0.005. This is an exploratory result on exposed evidence, not independent identification of particular meanings. The earlier taxonomy does not clearly beat its rivals, p=0.105.
- **The central missing validation:** expanded semantics predicts artifact type worse than sign identities and positional roles. Nested grouped validation gives log loss **0.7985 for semantics**, **0.7675 for roles**, and **0.7486 for sign identities**. Adding semantics to identities gives **0.7524**, with an interval including no gain. Useful grouping is not the same as a demonstrated decipherment.
- **A current reporting error:** retaining every unresolved sign lowers claimed second-corpus assigned-token coverage from **88.31% to 81.49%**, and fully assigned intact faces from **380/563 to 325/627**. Coverage is membership in a proposed inventory, not translation accuracy.

## 1. Is the system demonstrably linguistic, administrative, or something else?

It is demonstrably ordered and structured, and artifacts support administrative/economic uses for at least part of the record. Whether all inscriptions encode spoken language is not established by the present tests. Administrative use is compatible with linguistic writing, semasiographic records, emblematic identifiers or a mixture. Statistical structure does not by itself distinguish these possibilities. [Rao et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19666571/), [Sproat 2014](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2014.0031), [Kenoyer 2020](https://www.harappa.com/content/origin-and-development-indus-script-insights-harappa-and-other-sites).

## 2. Is there meaningful evidence favoring Proto-Dravidian over other families?

There is a reported lexical excess favoring the project's Dravidian dictionary **conditional on its selected visual and functional domains, normalization, dictionary construction and selection history**. Those assumptions matter. Recombining its stored p-values reproduces a very small Fisher reference p-value, but does not calibrate independence or correct the full adaptive search.

This continuation does not claim a fair new seven-family language contest. The required independent semantic constraints do not exist in the evaluated data. Unequal dictionary coverage and historical/reconstruction issues remain. Dravidian remains a plausible language hypothesis, not a quantified posterior or a demonstrated winner under the requested independent controls.

## 3. How much survives independent semantic constraints?

**No positive language-identification result in this continuation has genuinely independent semantic assignments behind it.** Therefore a numerical 'surviving fraction' of Dravidian evidence cannot honestly be estimated. Some archaeological structure survives without any words; that establishes function-related constraints, not a particular language. The expanded semantic-grouping result survives a specified rival-mapping control, but its semantic choices and evaluation evidence remain development-exposed.

## 4. Which sign meanings are best supported?

Broad quantitative and structural functions are better supported than exact words. Stroke-series signs plausibly encode quantities, although fixed-name, ordinal and classifier uses require separation. Cup-like 700 and signs such as 335, 480 and 171 warrant explicit quantity/unit tests; stroke adjacency and unit-chain behavior do not establish litre values or spoken names.

Sign 740 has a strong terminal/classifying role. Household, genitive, vessel designation and name/classifier interpretations remain distinguishable hypotheses, not synonyms for an established result. Copper-tablet associations for signs including 615, 407, 845 and 142 constrain context but do not identify loom, cloth, tax or blacksmith. Fish-shaped signs do not independently prove 'star'. The 45-sign table gives each proposal, rival senses, nonphonetic observations and the observation needed to split them.

## 5. Are any phonetic values independently established?

None is established by the examined evidence under the requested standard. This does not mean every proposed value is false. Recognizing a tally magnitude does not identify the word pronounced for it; pictorial resemblance, a plausible rebus and a dictionary hit are not an external phonetic anchor. Even Mahadevan distinguished an attractive fish/star hypothesis from proof. [Mahadevan's primary interview](https://www.harappa.com/content/indus-fish-sign).

## 6. Can a complete inscription be translated defensibly?

Conditional readings can be generated, and 60 additional real inscriptions are supplied with unchanged roots, ambiguous grammatical interpretations, predictions and controls. **None has an independently demonstrated complete translation in this evaluation.** No artificial words, catalogue IDs or successful semantic outcomes are supplied to fill that gap. A fluent paraphrase is especially weak evidence when its segmentation, case relations and lexical alternatives remain free.

## 7. Do newer results resolve the earlier decoy failures?

They do not yet resolve the failure to distinguish precise meanings from same-role alternatives. The preserved blind outcomes reproduce at **9/58 real versus 10/60 decoy**; both replay testers together give **28/57 versus 36/58**. The older constrained compound test is also null. New positive dictionary or broad-grouping statistics may coexist with these failures because they test different hypotheses.

The new expanded-taxonomy rival result deserves follow-up, but it does not certify individual values or override the older results. It needs independent semantic assignment, a full-search adversarial control, and new external targets.

## 8. What is genuinely discovered or new?

For this frozen mapping, the continuation adds a direct predictive ablation, matched semantic rivals, full-family archaeological controls, current-coverage corrections and explicit per-sign/inscription discriminators. The repo's linked corpus, hypothesis inventory and preserved nulls are useful resources.

Positional syntax, recurring phrases, Dravidian rebus proposals, artifact associations, allograph analysis and even geographical prediction without decipherment all have substantial precedents. In particular, [Oakes](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09296174.2017.1406294) studied sign–artifact/iconographic associations, and [Recchia and Louwerse](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12311) predicted provenance from inscription statistics. A novel implementation or more assigned signs is not a newly deciphered language. The literature review makes no global priority claim for this audit's methods.

## 9. What is the strongest next experiment?

Freeze semantic constraints assigned by archaeologists **without access to candidate-language readings**, then test the frozen key against hard same-role rivals on new source-authenticated archaeological outcomes. New residue/fibre, capacity, workshop or secure context measurements on already known objects can be valid new outcomes if genuinely withheld. Use whole-object/formula/context blocks and training-only model selection, with sign-only and context-only controls.

A forced bilingual/proper-name or phonetic-complement bridge would be especially decisive for sounds. Meanwhile, a full-method known-script calibration can directly test whether the inference procedure recovers correct values or falsely declares decipherment. The roadmap specifies all three priorities and the conditions that would change this verdict.

## Boundaries of the result

This is a reproducible continuation, not completion of a decipherment or independent authentication of every source object. Most original catalogue plates and dictionary entries remain to be collated; no untouched semantic holdout was found; no human panel has supplied blind judgments. The prepared cards and rating tasks are unscored. All such gaps are named in the audit and protocol instead of being replaced with simulated successes.
