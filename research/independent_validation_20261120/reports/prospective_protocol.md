# Independent semantic validation: protocol and three priorities

Status: **prepared, not run**. Freezing the current proposal after its development does not retroactively turn existing objects into a blind holdout. No independent archaeological panel has supplied labels, no experts have been contacted, and no new laboratory measurements have been made.

Update: a limited [known-Sumerian rebus component pilot](../rebus_positive_control/README.md) has now run. It is not completion of the full-method or archaeological protocol below. It finds correct original-order language ranking but unequal cluster filtering and an order-sensitive strict score.

## The decisive experiment

Ask whether **semantics assigned without knowledge of any candidate language permit the frozen Dravidian key to make specific correct predictions on independently collected evidence, outperforming equally flexible rival keys and language-free models**.

1. A curator inventories every dataset, object, photograph, context, corpus edition and derived file previously accessible to the project. Existing Lipi, projected Mahadevan, sem/events data, the new 60-row set and the current atlas are development evidence. Absence of a catalogue partner is not proof of non-exposure.
2. Reserve new evidence before anyone inspects it for this purpose: newly published securely excavated objects, previously unavailable excavator records, or genuinely new measurements on already known objects. The latter can be independent outcomes even when the sign sequences are familiar. Record exposure and collection dates. Keep the external outcome table inaccessible to all mapping developers.
3. At least three archaeologists/epigraphers independently receive neutral geometry, raw object/context records and structural summaries. They receive neither proposed roots nor English/German lexical domains. Collect probabilistic broad assignments and explicit alternatives, including unknown/identifier/classifier. Measure inter-rater agreement and publish disagreement. Familiarity with the signs can defeat blinding; the curator must disclose it. The generated 45 anonymous evidence cards are starting materials, not a completed blinded experiment.
4. Freeze those semantic constraints and their uncertainty **before** opening any language dictionary. In particular, a seal association cannot become 'chief', and a copper-tablet association cannot become 'tax', without a separate discriminating observation. Keep vessel/quantity/office/commodity/name alternatives live.
5. A separate language team tests Proto-Dravidian, Indo-Aryan/Indo-Iranian, Munda, Elamite, Sumerian, Burushaski and Akkadian, with an explicit unknown-language alternative. It must not see external test outcomes. Equalize admissible senses, dictionary-search budget, synonym count, phonological operations, morphology and candidate-selection rules. Document dictionary concept coverage and reconstruction uncertainty. A lexical item absent from a poorly documented family is missing evidence, not a linguistic failure.
6. Freeze a complete mapping, segmentation, morphology, alternative-reading probabilities and one primary predictive outcome. Disallow post-test rescue or silent translation changes; new proposals become a later version with a new holdout. Include candidate values for the *same* sign, such as household versus genitive/classifier for 740 and tax versus series/commodity for 845.
7. Score external outcomes using a proper rule, such as held-out log loss for independently recorded artifact function or a likelihood for quantitative measurements. Compare context-only, position/role-only, sign-identity, sign-order, compressed semantic and sign-plus-semantic models with training-only tuning. A deterministic meaning label cannot add information beyond its source sign; any benefit must reflect useful constraints/generalization, not a new data channel.
8. Keep entire physical objects, mould/batch groups, related text formulas and excavation contexts in the same evaluation block. Reserve whole sites or excavation campaigns where feasible. Report novel-object, novel-formula and novel-context results separately. Use block permutations or an explicit hierarchical sampling model, not independent-token tests.
9. Predeclare primary contrasts and familywise correction; release complete null scores, effect sizes and cluster uncertainty. Include every failed test. Report accuracy of individual values, not just assigned-token coverage. Calibrate the full selection procedure on known scripts with hidden readings and on meaningful nonphonological systems.
10. Only after semantic constraints and their predictions survive should the phonetic/compound result be treated as independent language evidence. A small, forced phonetic correspondence is more informative than a large fluent reading set.

## Priority 1 — New external semantic outcomes, including strong rivals

Build a modest collection with independent measurements that actually distinguish meanings. Good candidates include residues/fibres associated with sealings, securely linked container capacities, independently documented workshop contents, and context groups established without reading inscriptions. Use existing signs as predictors while withholding those newly measured outcomes. Do not measure only cases selected because they look favorable to the proposal.

For example, a textile reading for a tree sign predicts textile evidence more often than a matched nontextile commodity marker, within comparable object types, sites and preservation conditions. A tax reading requires a feature discriminating taxation from ownership, licensing, distribution or series identity. Merely predicting another administrative artifact is inadequate.

Define the smallest meaningful effect in advance and use realistic clustered simulations to choose the sample size. The current audit cannot supply a defensible universal required number: rare signs, few independent contexts and uncertain labels will dominate power. Publish a power curve, rather than promising that 50 inscriptions suffice.

## Priority 2 — Known-script calibration of the entire method

Hide the readings of administrative texts from a securely deciphered script. Give the method only the same kind of information available here: short sign sequences, shapes, object metadata and candidate dictionaries, with comparable missingness. Test whether it recovers the correct language and **individual signs**, not just an aggregate lexical excess. Refit descriptions, domains, candidate promotion and every allowed rescue in every decoy run.

Include meaning-bearing nonphonological records and wrong-language dictionaries. The critical outputs are false decipherment rate, sign-level precision, and calibration after equivalent search effort. This can falsify an overflexible inference method even without a new Indus bilingual.

## Priority 3 — A forced phonetic or quantitative bridge

Seek an independently identified proper name, phonetic complement, multilingual administrative equivalence or repeated quantitative conversion. An adequate bridge must force predictions on other objects while excluding alternative values, not merely resemble a proposed root.

For metrology, freeze the unit relation and uncertainty, predict capacities of unmeasured containers, and test competing bases and commodity-specific unit systems. Establishing a quantity or unit still leaves its spoken form unresolved. For sounds, require a repeated cross-script/name correspondence with independent provenance and direction, then test the same values elsewhere without retuning.

## Compositionality and blinded reading materials

The 120 prepared reading tasks mix proposed literal mappings with structure/frequency/complexity-matched alternatives. They contain no observed human scores. Fourteen constrained-decoy sequences remain textually identical to their proposals and must be treated as negative controls or excluded from discrimination scoring **before** raters begin. Do not count them as successful independent confirmations.

Collect exact morphological analyses, permitted insertions, segmentation alternatives and external predictions, not a global 'sounds coherent' judgment. Blind raters to the mapping key and sample order. Evaluate matched differences at inscription/formula level with corrections for all tested grammatical criteria. Expert preference for a grammatical Tamil phrase would test linguistic plausibility; archaeological outcomes are still needed to test whether that phrase is the ancient inscription's meaning.

## What would change the verdict

A predeclared mapping that predicts the new external evidence better than hard same-role rivals and sign-only baselines, with forced sign-level correspondences and calibrated known-script performance, would substantially strengthen the decipherment. A vocabulary that fits development examples but repeatedly fails these tests should lose its claim to established meanings, even if more dictionary matches can be found.
