Can vessel residues independently test the proposed words?

No verified same-object pairs were recovered. This is a completed feasibility check, with no new support for or against the proposed readings. The chemical data exist; the required inscription-to-vessel identities have not been established.

Three studies report 259 pottery samples. The main tables supply 209 archaeological sample rows. The Cambridge CSV additionally contains two modern references, excluded here, and lists 122 of the northwest study's 172 analysed fragments. Its recovered 42-page image register lists 49 low-yield IDs; one entry in the reported total remains unreconciled. These figures must not be presented as 259 independent labelled inscription tests.

The fixed food targets are rice (215/216), millet/grain (803/804/806/807), paddy (831/833), and sesame (854). None of the 13 catalogued inscribed pots from the shared sites carries these signs. The six target-bearing pottery records in the whole Lipi corpus are at Harappa or Mohenjo-daro, outside these three datasets' sites.

Additional source checked: the 2019 doctoral thesis includes five samples labelled Mohenjo-daro and five labelled Kalibangan. The author describes their teaching-collection provenance and excavation contexts as uncertain. These site labels yield another 159 catalogue-pottery candidates (three carrying food signs), but no verified object identity. They are kept separate from the three core studies; other overlapping thesis samples are not added again.

What is still needed

Recover a museum/excavation accession crosswalk or diagnostic photographs for a residue-tested inscribed pot. Alternatively, obtain analyses for inscribed target vessels. Then specify whether the inscription predicts contents, ownership or a transaction, and whether the chemical method can identify the proposed commodity.
